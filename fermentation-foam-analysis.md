## 4. Why Was This Beer So Foamy? A Complete Analysis

This NEIPA created a perfect storm of foam-positive factors. Every single variable was stacked in favor of maximum krausen production.

### 4.1 Protein Content: Maximum Foam Scaffolding

The grain bill is a foam machine:

- **Flaked oats (10.5%):** Oats are extremely high in protein (12-18%) and particularly rich in beta-glucans -- long-chain polysaccharides that dramatically increase wort viscosity. High-viscosity wort traps CO2 bubbles much more effectively, building taller, denser foam. The beta-glucans also create a gel-like matrix that stabilizes bubble walls.
- **Wheat malt (10.5%):** Wheat is high in protein (12-16%) and particularly rich in foam-positive proteins (LTP1 and protein Z). There is a reason wheat beers are famous for their foam. Wheat proteins are the primary structural component of beer foam.
- **Golden Promise (38.6%):** This is a high-protein barley malt (typically 10-11.5%) compared to standard pilsner malts. It contributes significant foam-positive protein.
- **21% adjunct grains (oats + wheat):** Combined, over one-fifth of the grist is high-protein, high-beta-glucan adjuncts. This is exceptionally high and creates a very protein-rich, viscous wort.

### 4.2 Raw Ale Technique: The Amplifier

The recipe name includes "Raw" -- this is a raw ale (no-boil or minimal-boil technique). This is arguably the single most important factor:

- **No hot break:** In a normal brew, boiling denatures and coagulates proteins, removing them as hot break. A raw ale skips this step, retaining ALL proteins in the wort -- including the large, foam-positive proteins that would normally be removed.
- **No protein coagulation:** Without boiling, the wort retains maximum beta-glucans, albumins, and globulins. These proteins are exceptional foam stabilizers.
- **Maximum viscosity:** Raw ale worts are noticeably thicker and more viscous than boiled worts due to retained proteins and beta-glucans. This viscosity traps CO2 and builds foam.
- **Unconverted starches:** Raw ales may retain some unconverted starches that contribute to viscosity and foam stability.

This is the factor that elevates this beer from "good krausen" to "extraordinary krausen."

### 4.3 Hop Compounds: Foam Stabilizers

- **300 grams of hops total** is a very heavy hopping rate for ~21 L.
- **Iso-alpha acids** (and even non-isomerized alpha acids from whirlpool/dry hops) are surface-active compounds. They migrate to the air-liquid interface of bubbles and stabilize them, preventing coalescence and collapse.
- **Hop oils and polyphenols** also contribute to foam stability. The three hop varieties used (Azacca, El Dorado, Sabro) are all high-alpha, high-oil varieties.
- **Sabro in particular** has very high oil content (~2.5-4.5 mL/100g) and contains unusual compounds (like coconut-flavored terpenes) that may contribute additional surface activity.
- The combination of hop-derived surface-active compounds with the protein matrix from the grain bill creates exceptionally stable foam.

### 4.4 Yeast Strain: Verdant IPA -- A Vigorous Top-Cropper

- **LalBrew Verdant IPA** is a well-known English-style ale yeast specifically selected for NEIPAs. It is a robust, vigorous fermenter.
- It is a **top-fermenting** strain that produces a large, active krausen as part of its normal behavior.
- Verdant IPA ferments aggressively in the 20-28 C range and produces significant CO2 volume during peak activity.
- English-heritage ale yeasts in general tend to produce larger, more persistent krausen than cleaner American ale strains.
- At 28 C, this yeast was at the very top of its tolerable range, pushing it into overdrive.

### 4.5 Pitch Temperature: 28 C -- Exceptionally Hot

- The wort was pitched at **28 C**, which is extremely warm even for an ale.
- Higher temperature = faster yeast metabolism = faster CO2 production = more foam.
- At 28 C, the yeast started fermenting almost immediately with no detectable lag phase.
- The temperature actually rose to **29.1 C** during peak fermentation due to the exothermic nature of fermentation itself -- the heat generated by the yeast's metabolic activity.
- The rate of CO2 production at 28-29 C is roughly 2-3x what it would be at a more typical 18-20 C.

### 4.6 OG and Fermentation Vigor

- **OG 1.071 (17.35 Plato)** is moderately high. More fermentable sugars = more CO2.
- **39 gravity points dropped in the first 24 hours** -- this means a huge volume of CO2 was produced in a very short time.
- The peak rate of 0.00365 SG/hour is extremely fast. For comparison, a typical ale fermentation peaks at around 0.001-0.002 SG/hour.
- All this CO2 had to go somewhere. In the FermZilla, it had to push through the liquid and the krausen layer, building up the foam.

### 4.7 FermZilla: The Closed Vessel Effect

- The FermZilla is a **pressurizable PET fermenter** with a relatively small dome/headspace above the liquid.
- **Limited headspace** means the krausen has nowhere to expand to. It gets compressed and pushed up into the dome.
- While the FermZilla likely had a blow-off tube (visible in photos), **any backpressure** -- even slight -- increases CO2 dissolution in the beer, which then nucleates more aggressively when it reaches the surface.
- The clear PET material makes the krausen dramatically visible. In an opaque stainless fermenter, the same krausen would exist but you would never see it.

### 4.8 Fermentable Sugar Profile

- A raw ale with a high proportion of base malts (79% pilsner + pale ale) likely has a very fermentable wort profile.
- Despite the oats and wheat (which add unfermentable dextrins), the 78% apparent attenuation confirms good fermentability.
- Highly fermentable worts undergo more vigorous initial fermentation because the simple sugars (maltose, glucose) are consumed rapidly in the first 24-48 hours.

### 4.9 Summary: The Perfect Foam Storm

| Factor | Contribution | Rating |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Raw ale (no boil) | Maximum retained proteins and beta-glucans | Extreme |
| Flaked oats + wheat (21%) | High protein, beta-glucans, viscosity | Very High |
| Golden Promise base | Higher protein than typical base malt | Moderate |
| 300g hops | Surface-active iso-alpha acids and oils | High |
| Verdant IPA yeast | Vigorous top-cropper | High |
| 28 C pitch temp | Turbo-charged fermentation rate | Very High |
| OG 1.071 | Lots of sugar to ferment = lots of CO2 | Moderate-High |
| FermZilla closed vessel | Compressed headspace, slight pressure | Moderate |

Every single factor was aligned for maximum foam. Remove any one of them and you would still have impressive krausen. Together, they produced the foam volcano seen in the photos.

## 5. Recipe: "Foam Monster" -- Maximum Krausen NEIPA (21 L)

A recipe explicitly designed to produce the most impressive fermentation foam possible, while still being a delicious, drinkable Hazy IPA.

### Design Philosophy

Take every lesson from the Raw NEIPA analysis and push each factor further, while keeping the beer balanced and tasty.

### Fermentables (6.5 kg total)

| Grain | Amount | % | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---|---------|
| Simpsons Golden Promise | 2.5 kg | 38.5% | High-protein base malt, bready flavor |
| BESTMALZ BEST Wheat | 1.2 kg | 18.5% | Maximum foam protein (LTP1, protein Z) |
| Flaked Oats | 1.2 kg | 18.5% | Beta-glucans, viscosity, silky mouthfeel |
| Flaked Wheat | 0.8 kg | 12.3% | Additional unmalted wheat protein (not denatured by kilning) |
| BESTMALZ BEST Heidelberg | 0.5 kg | 7.7% | Pilsner malt for light body and fermentability |
| Dextrose (table sugar) | 0.3 kg | 4.6% | Highly fermentable, drives fast initial fermentation |

**Total adjuncts: 49%** (wheat malt + flaked oats + flaked wheat). This is extreme but manageable with a raw ale technique since there is no sparge to get stuck.

**Why these choices:**
- **49% high-protein adjuncts** provides massive amounts of foam-positive proteins and beta-glucans.
- **Flaked wheat** (unmalted) retains even more raw protein than malted wheat.
- **Dextrose** is 100% fermentable and will be consumed first by the yeast, creating an initial burst of extremely vigorous CO2 production in the first 12 hours.
- **No crystal/caramel malts** -- we want maximum fermentability for the most aggressive initial fermentation.

**Estimated OG:** 1.068-1.072 | **Estimated FG:** 1.016-1.018 | **ABV:** ~7%

### Hops (350 g total)

| Hop | Amount | Timing | Purpose |
|-----|--------|--------|---------|
| Citra | 50 g | Mash hop (in the raw mash) | Oils and alpha acids in the wort from the start |
| Sabro | 50 g | Mash hop | High oil content, surface-active compounds |
| Citra | 50 g | Whirlpool / post-mash steep at 80C | Hop oils, flavor |
| Sabro | 50 g | Whirlpool / post-mash steep at 80C | Hop oils, flavor |
| El Dorado | 50 g | Dry hop at HIGH KRAUSEN (Day 1) | Nucleation + biotransformation + foam stabilization |
| Citra | 50 g | Dry hop at high krausen (Day 1) | Same -- this is the critical timing |
| Sabro | 50 g | Dry hop at high krausen (Day 1) | Same |

**Why these choices:**
- **Mash hops** ensure hop-derived surface-active compounds are present from the very beginning of fermentation, stabilizing the first bubbles.
- **Dry hops at high krausen (Day 1)** is the key trick. Adding dry hops during active fermentation causes explosive CO2 nucleation (the hop pellets act as nucleation sites). This is the famous "dry hop volcano" effect. The hop oils also stabilize the existing foam.
- **Sabro** specifically has one of the highest total oil contents of any commercial hop variety, making it excellent for foam stabilization.
- **350 g total** is aggressive but not wasteful -- the beer will be intensely hoppy.

### Yeast

| Yeast | Amount | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| LalBrew Verdant IPA | 2 packets (22 g) | Over-pitch slightly for fastest possible start |

**Why:**
- Verdant IPA is proven in the original batch as a vigorous top-cropper.
- **Two packets** (slight over-pitch) ensures the yeast starts fast with zero lag phase. The faster fermentation begins, the more violent the initial CO2 production.
- An alternative would be **Lallemand London ESB** (another aggressive English top-cropper) or a Kveik strain (Voss), but Verdant IPA has the proven track record for krausen volume.

### Water

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Mash water | ~20 L (per the mash water calculation: pre-boil volume minus grain kg) |
| No sparge | Raw ale technique |

### Process: Raw Ale (No Boil)

1. **Mash at 65 C for 60 minutes** with all grains and mash hops
2. **Vorlauf** until clear-ish, then drain to kettle
3. **Heat to 80 C** (pasteurization, NOT boiling), add whirlpool hops, steep 20 min
4. **Chill to 30 C** -- yes, deliberately warm
5. **Pitch yeast at 30 C** -- this is the secret weapon for maximum foam
6. **Allow free rise to 32 C** during the first 24 hours -- do not cool
7. **Day 1 (12-18 hours after pitch):** Add dry hops directly into the fermenter during peak krausen. Stand back.
8. **Day 2 onwards:** Allow temperature to naturally fall to 22-24 C
9. **Day 5-7:** Cold crash to 2-4 C
10. **Day 7-10:** Package

### Mash Water Calculation

Per the brewing preference: mash_water_L = pre_boil_volume_L - total_grain_kg
- Pre-boil volume (target into fermenter for raw ale): ~25 L
- Total grain: 6.2 kg (excluding dextrose, which goes directly into wort)
- Mash water = 25 - 6.2 = **18.8 L**
- Dissolve dextrose in the hot wort post-mash

### Expected Fermentation Profile

Based on the analysis of the original batch, scaled up:

- **Hours 0-6:** Visible fermentation begins. Krausen starts forming.
- **Hours 6-12:** Krausen grows rapidly. Foam reaches top of vessel.
- **Hours 12-18:** Add dry hops. FOAM EXPLOSION. Krausen erupts. Blow-off tube absolutely essential.
- **Hours 18-36:** Peak foam. Expect significant blowoff losses (plan for 10-15% loss). SG drops 35-45 points.
- **Hours 36-48:** Foam begins to recede. Most fermentation complete.
- **Day 3-5:** Cleanup phase. Gravity stabilizes around 1.016-1.018.

### Flavor Profile

Despite being optimized for foam, this will be a genuinely delicious beer:
- **Hazy, juicy NEIPA** with tropical fruit (mango, pineapple, coconut from Sabro)
- **Silky, creamy mouthfeel** from the oats and wheat
- **Full body** with moderate sweetness from the high FG
- **Intense hop aroma** from the 350g of premium hops
- The raw ale technique preserves delicate malt flavors that are normally driven off by boiling

## 6. Tips and Tricks for Maximizing Fermentation Foam

### Temperature Management
- **Pitch warm (28-32 C)** for maximum initial fermentation speed. This is the single easiest variable to control.
- **Do not cool** during the first 24 hours. Let the yeast generate its own heat. Free rise to 30+ C is ideal for foam (though it will produce more esters -- which is fine for a NEIPA).
- Warm fermentation = 2-3x faster CO2 production compared to cool fermentation.

### Grain Bill Composition
- **Use at least 20% high-protein adjuncts** (flaked oats, flaked wheat, wheat malt, spelt, rye).
- **Flaked oats** are the MVP -- they contribute both protein and beta-glucans. Beta-glucans are the key to dense, persistent, meringue-like krausen (as opposed to thin, bubbly foam).
- **Flaked wheat** (unmalted) retains more protein than malted wheat.
- **Avoid mashing too hot** -- you want fermentable wort (lots of CO2) rather than dextrinous wort.

### Raw Ale / No-Boil Technique
- **This is the biggest amplifier.** Skipping the boil retains all proteins that would normally be removed as hot break.
- If you do boil, keep it short (15-20 min) and skip whirlfloc/Irish moss to retain more protein.
- Never use kettle finings (whirlfloc, Irish moss, carrageenan) if you want maximum foam.

### Yeast Selection
- Choose **vigorous top-fermenting English ale strains**: Verdant IPA, London ESB, Nottingham.
- **Kveik strains** (especially Voss) ferment at 35-40 C and produce enormous krausen.
- **Over-pitch slightly** (1.5-2x normal rate) to eliminate lag time and get the fastest possible start.
- Rehydrate dry yeast properly for fastest activation.
- Avoid lager yeasts, Belgian yeasts that are slow starters, or low-flocculation American strains.

### Hop Timing
- **Dry hop during active fermentation (Day 1, at high krausen).** Hop pellets provide millions of nucleation sites that cause dissolved CO2 to suddenly come out of solution -- this is the "dry hop volcano" effect.
- **More hops = more nucleation sites = more foam.** Use at least 200-300g for a 21 L batch.
- **Mash hops or first wort hops** ensure surface-active hop compounds are in the wort from the start.
- High-oil hop varieties (Sabro, Simcoe, Citra, Mosaic) contribute more surface-active compounds.

### Vessel Choice
- **Closed vessels** (FermZilla, stainless uni-tanks) compress the krausen and make it more dramatic.
- **Small headspace** forces foam upward. Fill the vessel to 75-80% capacity.
- A blow-off tube is ESSENTIAL -- airlock will clog and blow off the lid.
- **Clear vessels** (FermZilla, glass carboy) let you see and photograph the krausen.
- Avoid wide, open-top fermenters -- the krausen spreads out and looks less impressive.

### Wort Composition
- **Add simple sugars** (dextrose, table sugar, honey) at 5-10% of the grist. These ferment first and fastest, creating the initial CO2 burst.
- **High OG (1.065-1.080)** provides more fuel for CO2 production. Much higher and the yeast may be stressed.
- Ensure adequate yeast nutrients if using a high percentage of sugar.

### Pitch Rate and Oxygenation
- **Over-pitch by 50-100%** for fastest possible fermentation start.
- **Oxygenate well** -- healthy yeast with plenty of oxygen reproduce faster, build more biomass, and ferment more vigorously.
- Alternatively, under-pitching by 50% with heavy oxygenation creates a longer lag phase but then an extremely violent fermentation once the yeast population catches up. This is riskier.

### Advanced Tricks
- **Rice hulls** as a mash filter aid if using very high percentages of flaked adjuncts (over 30%).
- **Alpha-amylase enzyme** (0.5 mL) added at mashing can increase fermentability, leading to more vigorous fermentation and more CO2.
- **Time your dry hop addition precisely** -- watch the krausen, and add the hops right when it's at its peak height (usually 12-18 hours after pitch at warm temperatures).
- **Pressure fermentation** at low PSI (5-10 PSI) in a FermZilla or similar vessel increases CO2 dissolution, which then nucleates more violently when disturbed (e.g., by dry hop addition).

### What NOT to Do
- Do not use kettle finings (whirlfloc, Irish moss) -- these remove foam-positive proteins.
- Do not use a long, vigorous boil -- this removes proteins as hot break.
- Do not ferment cold (below 18 C) -- slower fermentation = less dramatic foam.
- Do not use low-attenuation yeast -- you need maximum CO2 production.
- Do not use a wide-open fermenter -- the foam spreads out instead of building up.
- Do not add anti-foam agents (obviously).
