# IPA Batches Ranked by Actual BU/GU

All **completed** IPA-family batches that have a **measured OG**, ranked by actual BU/GU (descending). Generated 2026-08-07.

## Method

- **Scope:** batches with `status = Completed` (excludes the one currently fermenting and one archived batch)
- **Styles included:** American IPA, Hazy IPA (New England / NEIPA), Hazy IPA, Double IPA, Black IPA, White IPA
- **Filter:** must have a `measuredOg` recorded
- **BU/GU = IBU ÷ GU**, where **GU = (measured OG − 1.000) × 1000**

The IBU figure is Brewfather's calculated value (Tinseth) — IBU is not directly measurable at home, so this is necessarily an estimate. What makes this list "actual" rather than "recipe" is the **gravity side**: GU comes from the measured OG on brew day, not the recipe target. Where brewhouse efficiency ran high or low, the actual BU/GU differs from the recipe's planned figure.

The rightmost column shows Brewfather's own `estimatedBuGuRatio` for comparison — it matches the computed value closely in almost every case, which is a good consistency check.

## Observations

### The range is enormous — 0.43 to 1.61

Nearly a 4× spread in bitterness-to-gravity across your IPAs. That's not inconsistency; it's a deliberate stylistic split, and it maps almost perfectly onto style:

### Clear style clustering

| Group | BU/GU range | Batches |
|---|---|---|
| **Aggressive / old-school** | 1.06 – 1.61 | #2, #19, #53, #21, #51, #11 |
| **Classic West Coast** | 0.64 – 0.96 | #60, #15, #91, #22, #46, #32, #87, #61, #79, #57, #55, #77 |
| **Hazy / juicy** | 0.43 – 0.63 | #85, #65, #78, #64, #69, #47, #8, #6 |

The **NEIPA/Hazy batches occupy almost the entire bottom third** of the list (8 of the last 9 entries), while the high end is dominated by **Black IPAs, White IPAs, and the Double IPA**. That's exactly the right instinct — hazies should be low-BU/GU (soft, juicy, chloride-forward) and West Coast styles should sit at 0.7-1.0.

### Your NEIPAs are correctly restrained

All eight hazy batches land between **0.43 and 0.63**, well inside the modern NEIPA convention of 0.4-0.6. No hazy has ever crept above 0.63. Good discipline — over-bittering is the classic way to ruin a hazy.

### The current batch in context

**#91 Double IPA 7.5 kg (BU/GU 0.92)** sits at rank 10 — firmly in the "classic West Coast" band despite being a Double IPA. For comparison, your other Double IPAs are #2 at 1.61 (very aggressive, 2023) and #79 at 0.68 (smooth, 2026). #91 lands neatly between them, which matches the design intent discussed while building it: aggressive but not a bitterness bomb.

### Highest-gravity IPA ≠ most bitter

**#7 Doppio** has by far the highest measured OG (1.0958, GU 95.8) but ranks 21st at BU/GU 0.58 — 56 IBU spread across a very large gravity reads as balanced rather than bitter. Conversely **#2 White Double IPA** at a modest OG 1.0534 carries 86 IBU for a BU/GU of 1.61. Bitterness perception tracks the *ratio*, not absolute IBU.

## Caveats

- **IBU is calculated, not measured.** Real IBU depends on hop age, storage, utilisation, and whirlpool contribution that formulas model imperfectly. Whirlpool-heavy and dry-hop-heavy beers (most of your hazies) often measure higher in the lab than Tinseth predicts.
- **BU/GU uses OG, not FG.** It measures bitterness against *starting* gravity. Two beers with identical BU/GU but different FG will taste differently — the drier one tastes more bitter. Worth pairing this with attenuation data for a fuller picture.
- **Style tags are self-assigned** and a few are loose (#11 "Sour Tropical Stout" tagged Black IPA, #22 a braggot tagged NEIPA). The filter follows the tags as recorded in Brewfather.

## What changes between the two rankings

Most batches sit in a similar place on both lists, but a few move sharply — and those movements are the interesting part.

### Biggest riser going from BU/GU to absolute IBU

**#7 Doppio: rank 21 → rank 10.** At 56 IBU it's the 10th most bitter beer you've brewed in absolute terms, but because it's also your highest-gravity beer ever (OG 1.0958, 10.2% ABV) the bitterness is spread thin — BU/GU 0.58 puts it near the bottom. This is the clearest illustration of why both numbers matter: Doppio has real bitterness, it just has even more malt to balance it.

### Biggest faller

**#21 Survivable Amber IPA: rank 4 → rank 9**, and **#53 Galaxy West Coast 2: rank 3 → rank 7.** Both are modest-gravity beers (OG ~1.056) carrying 60-62 IBU. High ratio, moderate absolute bitterness — they *taste* aggressively bitter without being IBU monsters.

### Unchanged at the top

**#2 White Double IPA is #1 on both lists** — 86 IBU at OG 1.0534. It's simultaneously your most bitter beer in absolute terms and the most bitter relative to gravity. At 5.8% ABV with 86 IBU, this must have been genuinely punishing to drink.

### The hazies stay at the bottom either way

All 10 Hazy/NEIPA batches occupy the bottom 9 positions on the IBU list (only #32 lupomax neipa 33 at 51 IBU escapes, sitting 13th). Whether you measure absolutely or relatively, your hazies are consistently the least bitter — as they should be.

### #91 in context

Your most recent big beer, **#91 Double IPA 7.5 kg**, is **4th most bitter of all time at 68 IBU** while only 9th by BU/GU (0.92). That combination — high absolute bitterness, moderate ratio — is exactly the classic Double IPA balance point. It's a genuinely bitter beer that has the malt to carry it.

## Which metric to use

- **BU/GU** predicts *perceived balance* — whether the beer tastes bitter-forward or malt-forward. Better for judging whether a recipe is in style.
- **Absolute IBU** predicts *total bitterness load* — how much iso-alpha-acid is actually in the glass. Better for comparing hop bills and hop cost.

For style conformity, use BU/GU. For "how hoppy is this beer really," use IBU. Both are calculated (Tinseth) on the bitterness side, so both inherit the same estimation caveats noted above.
