# Nottingham (LalBrew Nottingham) Fermentation Report

Analysis of 10 batches fermented with LalBrew Nottingham yeast that have RAPT Pill hydrometer data from Brewfather. Three additional Nottingham batches were excluded for lack of sensor data: #97 Wethop Blonde 2026 (currently fermenting, only one reading so far), #41 imp. m. stout 2/3, and #14 Golden ale 2 (no readings).

Apparent attenuation and ABV are calculated from **measured** OG/FG (Brewfather brew-day values), which are more reliable than the Pill's absolute readings. Peak timing and rate come from the Pill telemetry.

## Temperature Influence

LalBrew spec Nottingham for 16.7-22.2C, but as with the other yeasts in this cellar it was almost always pitched warm (24-28C). Key observations:

1. **Nottingham is vigorous and heat-tolerant.** Warm pitches of 27-29C (#76, #43, #71-pre-crash) produced fast, clean ferments with no stalling. This contrasts sharply with BRY-97, which stalled at 70% attenuation when pitched at 29-30C (batches #46, #39). Nottingham did not show that failure mode in any batch here.

2. **Fast peak, early finish.** Warm batches peaked at 15-29h and were effectively terminal within 2-3 days. The day-1 and day-2 gravity drops are large (often 0.025-0.046), bigger single-day drops than BRY-97 typically shows.

3. **Strong attenuator.** Mean 82% apparent attenuation, frequently meeting or exceeding the 80-81% spec, and reaching 93% on the amber IPA (#21). Only the dextrin-heavy stout (#74, 75%) and the wheat beer (#63, 76%) finished notably below spec, as expected from their grists.

4. **Cold fermentation works too.** #71, held at ~12C, fermented slowly and steadily over a week to 83% attenuation — clean, no stall. Nottingham's reputation for cold tolerance holds up in the data.

## Nottingham vs BRY-97 vs Verdant IPA

| Metric | Nottingham (n=6/10) | BRY-97 (n=15) | Verdant IPA (n=7) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak timing (mean) | **22.3h** | 25.9h | 19.6h |
| Peak timing (median) | **23.3h** | 25.2h | 18.9h |
| Peak rate (mean) | **0.00306** | 0.00239 | 0.00276 |
| Peak rate (median) | **0.00304** | 0.00235 | 0.00265 |
| Completion (median) | **~48-60h** | 72h | 56h |
| Attenuation (mean) | **82.0%** | 79.2% | 79% |
| Attenuation (median) | **80.7%** | 80.6% | 78% |
| Typical pitch temp | 24-28C | 22-26C | 22-25C |

**Nottingham is the most vigorous of the three** — the highest mean and median peak rate (0.00306 SG/h), driven by very large day-1/day-2 drops. It peaks sooner than BRY-97 (~22h vs ~26h), though not quite as early as Verdant.

**It is also the highest attenuator** on average (82% mean), edging out both BRY-97 and Verdant by ~3 points, and it reached the single highest attenuation in any report (93%, #21).

**It finishes fast.** Warm Nottingham batches were terminal in 2-2.5 days, quicker than BRY-97's ~3 days and comparable to Verdant.

The standout practical difference is **heat tolerance**: BRY-97 stalled when pitched hot, but Nottingham shrugged off 27-29C pitches and even fermented cleanly at 12C. It is the most forgiving and flexible of the three strains in this dataset.

## Data Quality Notes

- **RAPT Pill readings during cold crash are unreliable.** As in the other reports, several batches show small SG spikes or dips once temperature drops below ~5C (e.g. #21's dip to 0.985). These were ignored.
- **#43 Hoppy English Brown Ale** and **#3 light golden ale** had outright Pill malfunctions (SG values of 0.38-3.97) late in their runs; only the early, physically plausible readings were used, and peak/done timing was taken from the daily tables.
- **#31 Golden Ale #3** had its Pill start recording ~2 days after pitch (first reading 1.027 vs measured OG 1.049), so OG capture and peak timing are missing; attenuation is from measured OG/FG.
- **#71 Wet hop blonde hybrid** is a legitimate cold/slow ferment (~12C), not a stall — its low "peak rate" is an artifact of the spread-out, even gravity drop.
- Apparent attenuation and ABV throughout use **measured** brew-day OG/FG rather than Pill absolute values, which run a few points off (the Pill tends to read OG low and can drift during active, CO2-rich fermentation).
